Is (S)-crizotinib in BindingDB as its own compound, separate from crizotinib? Compare the two enantiomers on the targets where both were measured.

🧠 Assistant

Yes. (S)-crizotinib is its own record, BDBM50352564, separate from crizotinib, which is the (R)-enantiomer (BDBM50306682). BindingDB also has a third record of the same molecule drawn without its stereocentre (BDBM179803).

Data: BindingDB release 202610. I took the SMILES of each form from PubChem (via chemical_name_to_smiles) and ran an exact structure search on each:

Query Exact matches Record Measurements Targets
(R)-crizotinib, [C@@H] 1 BDBM50306682 (CRIZOTINIB) 813 402
(S)-crizotinib, [C@H] 1 BDBM50352564 (CHEMBL1825141) 13 4
No stereo given 1 BDBM179803 (compound 11a) 10 6

Each query finds exactly one record. Exact search compares canonical isomeric SMILES, so the configuration of the one stereocentre is part of the match. A similarity search at threshold 1.0 is different: it ignores stereo and returns all three records with similarity 1.0. Use exact search when the form matters, and similarity at 1.0 (or the InChIKey first block, KTEIFNKAUNYNJU) to collect every form.

On the drug's own targets, MET and ALK, the approved (R)-enantiomer is two orders of magnitude more potent. PMID 21812414 is the 2011 paper that reported crizotinib's discovery, and it measured the (S)-enantiomer only on MET.

MTH1 is the exception, and BindingDB can show only half of it. Most of the (S)-enantiomer's data are on MTH1 (NUDT1, an enzyme that sanitizes oxidized nucleotides): Kd 39 nM and IC50 138–1,800 nM across five papers (2016–2020). (S)-crizotinib is known as an MTH1 inhibitor, but BindingDB has no MTH1 measurement for (R)-crizotinib, so it cannot show the reversal itself. That needs the primary literature.

The third record is neither enantiomer. BDBM179803 is drawn without stereo, so it is either the racemate or a compound whose configuration the source did not record. Its values (MET IC50 14.6 nM and ALK IC50 17.6 nM in PMID 26950400, plus MET, ALK and JAK2 values from a patent and three other papers) cannot be assigned to either enantiomer. An exact search for crizotinib misses them, which is usually what you want.

Practical rules

  • To check whether one specific form (an enantiomer, a salt, a labelled compound) is registered, and to get only its data, use exact structure search with a stereo-specific SMILES.
  • To gather every form of a compound, use similarity search at 1.0 or match the first 14 characters of the InChIKey (Example 1.2).
  • To compare enantiomers, compare within one paper and one assay. Across papers the spread is large: (R)-crizotinib alone has 50 MET IC50 values from dozens of papers, with a median of 8 nM.
