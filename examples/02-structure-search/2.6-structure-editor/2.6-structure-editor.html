Two things to keep in mind:

  • Partial search: the search covered only 100,000 of the 230,323 compounds and stopped at 50 rows, so stronger binders may be among the compounds it didn't reach. Rerunning with a higher limit, or adding an affinity cutoff such as ≤ 0.2 nM, would confirm the ranking.

The lesson: the editor builds the query, and the assistant reads the results. The structure editor is the hands-on way to set up a structure search: draw or load a molecule, choose exact, substructure or similarity, and set an affinity window. The search runs inside the editor, so the assistant does not see the results automatically. When you follow up ("render the top 5"), it fetches them with get_editor_results and continues from there. Two habits help:

  • Check the search coverage. Editor searches are capped (here: 50 rows, 100,000 compounds scanned). For a complete ranking, ask the assistant to rerun the same query with substructure_search and a tighter affinity cutoff.
  • Check the record behind a striking value. The top hit's 0.35 pM Ki sits on a target record with a yeast organism (S. cerevisiae) and the name "GSP1/CNR1", which almost certainly stands for the CB1 receptor. Rimonabant, a close relative, binds rat CB1 at 0.31 nM (rank 10). A 1,000-fold stronger value on a mislabelled target is more likely a curation error than a breakthrough.
