Analyze the patent landscape in BindingDB.

🧠 Assistant

Data: BindingDB release 202611. A record counts as a patent when its source ID starts with US or WO.

Scale

  • 9,004 patents: 7,262 granted US patents (B1/B2), 1,640 US applications (1,631 A1, 8 A9, 1 A2), 24 US reissues and 78 WO/PCT applications. Almost all are US documents published from 2013 on, when BindingDB started curating patents.
  • 1.36 M measurements (42% of BindingDB) and 699 k distinct compounds (48% of the 1.45 M compounds with a measurement), covering 3,224 of the 12,002 targets with data.
  • Patent chemical space barely overlaps the literature. Only 25 k patent compounds (3.6%) also appear in a journal article or other non-patent source. In practice, patents are a separate, mostly unpublished chemistry collection.
  • Since 2015, patents add more compounds per year than journal articles do: 47–91 k per year against about 24–36 k (Figure 1). The drop in articles after 2023 is curation lag, not a real decline.
  • A typical patent reports 42 compounds (median; 90th percentile 247; maximum 2,689 in US12581252-B2, PI3Kα). 60% of patents report data on only one target.

What is being patented

For each patent I took its lead target (the one with the most compounds measured, pooling mutants and constructs by UniProt accession), so that safety-panel targets don't inflate the counts. hERG (119 patents) and CYP3A4 (108) appear in more than 100 patents each, but almost always as off-targets.

Most-patented lead targets overall: BTK (178), JAK2 (163), EGFR (104), BACE1 (103), BRD4 (96), PI3Kδ (91), LSD1 (86), RORγt (82), IRAK4 (80), Nav1.7 (74), FGFR1 and JAK1 (68 each). The JAK family as a whole (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, TYK2) is the lead target of 291 patents. If every target measured counts, the JAK family leads (JAK2 246, JAK1 234, JAK3 195, TYK2 152), along with BTK (226) and EGFR (156).

A caution on the lead-target split: in 1,760 patents (20%) two or more targets tie for the most compounds, typically JAK panels that test every compound on JAK1, JAK2, JAK3 and TYK2. Ties go to the target with more measurements, then to the lower UniProt accession, which favours JAK2 (O60674) over JAK1 (P23458). So read JAK2 vs JAK1 as "JAK family", not as a real preference.

Class mix (Figure 2):

  • Kinases are the largest single class and steady to growing: 29% of patents in 2013–17, 32% in 2022–26 (33% in 2025).
  • GPCRs fell from 20% (2013) to 7% (2025), and proteases from about 10% to 4–5%.
  • Epigenetic targets rose from about 2% to 7–9% by 2017–2022 and are around 6–8% now.
  • Small but rising classes: E3 ligase / degrader patents (cereblon), phosphatases (almost all SHP2) and immuno-oncology targets. The immuno-oncology group (PD-L1, IDO1, STING, CD73) peaked at 5.9% in 2022 and has declined since (2.7% in 2025).

Rising and falling targets (Figure 3)

Comparing 2013–17 (2,752 patents) with 2022–26 (3,425 patents):

  • Newly crowded: HPK1/MAP4K1 (0 → 44 patents), SARS-CoV-2 pp1a/Mpro (0 → 41) and pp1ab (0 → 31), KRAS (0 → 41), RIPK1 (1 → 35), SHP2 (3 → 35), small-molecule PD-1/PD-L1 (0 → 31), cereblon (0 → 29, plus 19 for the DDB1–cereblon complex), 5-HT2A (9 → 38, largely psychedelic-derived agonists for CNS indications), STING (0 → 23), RET (2 → 25), IRAK4 (11 → 36), DGKα (0 → 21) and TYK2 (1 → 19).
  • Largely abandoned: BACE1 (80 → 1) and PDE10A (53 → 3) after their clinical failures, plus Nav1.7 (40 → 8), SYK (28 → 6), RORγt (29 → 9), OX1R (24 → 5) and OX2R (19 → 6), S1P1 (19 → 2), PIM1 (19 → 4), c-MET (22 → 8), VEGFR2 (23 → 9) and PI3Kδ (33 → 19). BTK is still the largest lead target but has lost share (56 → 50).

Title and abstract keyword trends (2013–17 → 2022–26): degrader/PROTAC 8 → 107, KRAS 0 → 53, deuterated compounds 6 → 70, macrocycles 23 → 63, COVID/coronavirus 0 → 80. Pain (198 → 97) and psychiatric (197 → 96) patents roughly halved, while oncology patents grew from 741 to 1,201.

Saturation by target. Among the 171 targets with at least 3,000 compounds, patents now hold almost all of the known chemistry for several: 93% of HPK1 compounds come from patents, 91% for IRAK4, 90% for RIPK1, 88% for KRAS and TYK2, and 96–100% for PD-L1, complement factor D, DGKα/ζ and SARS-CoV-2 pp1a. Classic academic targets are the opposite: acetylcholinesterase, butyrylcholinesterase, carbonic anhydrases I, IX and XII, the D2 dopamine, μ-opioid and adenosine A1 receptors are each 2% or less patent-derived.

Potency and molecular properties (Figure 4)

Patent data are about 0.6–1.3 log units more potent than literature data (exact values only): median pIC50 7.2 vs 6.4, pKi 7.6 vs 7.0, pKd 7.7 vs 6.4 and pEC50 7.2 vs 6.4. 26% of patent IC50 values are 10 nM or better, compared with 15% in the literature. This reflects the bias of optimized patent series and claim-supporting examples.

Patent molecules are getting larger. The share above 500 Da rose from 24% (2014) to 43% (2025), and the median MW from 438 to 482. Patent compounds are also larger than literature compounds from the same period (2013–2023: median MW 462 vs 422), though literature compounds are growing too (median 400 in 2013, 434 in 2022). Degraders, macrocycles and KRAS/PPI chemistry drive the increase.

Caveats

  • BindingDB holds no assignee data, only inventor names, so this analysis cannot rank companies.
  • Coverage is essentially US-only and selective: these are patents BindingDB chose to curate, not the full patent record.
  • Patent "year" is the publication or grant year, so trends lag filing by 2–4 years. 2026 is a partial year.
  • Target classes come from keyword rules on target names, which are approximate (about a fifth to a third of patents fall in "Other enzymes & proteins": autotaxin, PARP1, KRAS, IDH1, BCL-2 and the like). Lead targets are ambiguous in the 20% of patents with ties (see above).

What changed since the 202610 version of this example

The headline picture is the same (patents are 42% of measurements, a separate chemistry collection, more potent and larger molecules, kinases first, BACE1 and PDE10A abandoned, HPK1, KRAS, SHP2, cereblon and SARS-CoV-2 rising). Some numbers moved because of how they were computed here: lead targets now pool mutants and constructs by UniProt accession with a deterministic tie-break (which moves EGFR and JAK2 up the lead-target list, and FGFR1 and HDAC1 down), kinase classes now include receptor tyrosine kinases whose names lack the word "kinase" (EGFR, FGFR, VEGFR, c-MET, TRKA), so kinases are about 30% rather than 23–28%, and potency medians use exact values only, which narrows the patent-vs-literature gap from about 1 log unit to 0.6–0.8 for IC50 and Ki. KRAS (0 → 41 lead-target patents) now enters the rising list.

How this was answered

Sixteen run_sql queries on activity (with article for titles and abstracts and compound_property for molecular weight): patent kinds, scale and overlap, per-year documents and compounds, compounds per patent, lead targets (one row per patent and UniProt accession, then the top one per patent), class mix, rising and falling targets, keyword trends, patent share per target, potency and molecular weight. The figures were drawn with matplotlib from those results (plot_patent_landscape.py).
