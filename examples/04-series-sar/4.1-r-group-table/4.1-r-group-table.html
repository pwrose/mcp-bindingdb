👤 User

Celecoxib is a 1,5-diarylpyrazole. Tabulate that whole series against COX-2 for me, by R-group position, and tell me what each position prefers.

🧠 Assistant

The series is in BindingDB in force. Against human COX-2 (target 2262, P35354) 366 of the 4,557 compounds with an exact IC50 contain the 1,5-diarylpyrazole core, and the decomposition gives three positions: R1, the para position of the aryl on pyrazole N1; R2, the pyrazole C3 substituent; R3, the aryl at C5. The table below is the top of the series at the two positions the medicinal chemistry fixed — a sulfonamide at R1 and a trifluoromethyl at R2 — so that what varies down it is R3 alone.

R1 is not a variable in this series so much as its definition. 251 of the 366 compounds carry a primary sulfonamide there (median 339 nM, best 0.45 nM) and 32 a methylsulfone (median 902 nM, best 45 nM). Everything else is rare and mediocre: hydrogen in 20 compounds (median 2.0 µM), methyl in 13 (1.2 µM), chloro in 11 (759 nM), fluoro in 10 (219 nM), methoxy in 9 (955 nM), bromo in 9 (219 nM). The next answer is about what those few non-sulfonamides are actually doing, because they are not simply weaker.

R2 wants the trifluoromethyl, and the medians say otherwise. 98 compounds have CF₃ (median 525 nM, best 0.447 nM) and 14 have CHF₂ (median 80 nM, best 10 nM) — on medians the difluoromethyl looks three times better, but the medians compare different compound sets. The three pairs that differ only at R2 say the opposite, every time:

R3 CF₃ CHF₂
4-chlorophenyl 0.45 nM 10 nM
4-methylphenyl (celecoxib) 0.52 nM 12.9 nM
4-methoxyphenyl 7.94 nM 15 nM

The CHF₂ compounds are a small, late, well-optimised subset; the CF₃ set includes everything that was ever made. That is the usual reason a median flips a conclusion, and matched compounds are the cure.

R2 also collects compounds that are not coxibs at all. 30 of the 366 hang a flavone (a chrysin ether-ester) off that position and 20 a chlorocoumarin acylhydrazone — whole chemotypes that happen to contain a 1,5-diarylpyrazole, with medians of 1.95 and 5.4 µM. A marked core is a structural filter, not a claim about series membership; reading the counts without looking at what the groups are would have made the pyrazole look far more tolerant at C3 than it is.

R3 is the position that was actually optimised, and the table shows its ladder. A bare phenyl (60 compounds, median 944 nM) reaches only 32 nM at its best; one small para substituent buys about two orders of magnitude — 4-chloro (24 compounds) gives SC-58236 at 0.45 nM and 4-methyl (27 compounds) celecoxib at 0.52 nM. Larger or more polar para groups fall off gently: dimethylamino 4.7 nM, methoxy 7.9, methylthio 9.0, bromo 9.3, fluoro 40.7. Adding a second substituent to that ring does not help (3-methyl-4-methylthio 3.7 nM against 9.0 for the plain methylthio is the only gain; 3,4-dichloro is 15 nM against 0.45 for 4-chloro alone).

On the numbers. Each bar is that compound's most potent exact IC50 in nM, pooled over every source in BindingDB, which is what the SAR tools rank on. For celecoxib that single number comes from 225 measurements — the third answer is about what it hides.

How this was answered

  1. search_targets for "cyclooxygenase" in Homo sapiens gave COX-2 (target 2262, 5,518 compounds) and COX-1 (7854, 2,863).
  2. decompose_r_groups with the 1,5-diarylpyrazole core, marked at all three positions, target_id: 2262 and max_compounds: 5000 returned the 366 matching compounds, their R-groups and their IC50, plus the per-position summary (summarize, on by default) that the counts and medians above come from.
  3. draw_r_group_table drew the core with its positions named and the eight compounds, taking the cells straight from the decomposition's rows.

👤 User

Does one of those positions decide COX-1 versus COX-2 selectivity?

🧠 Assistant

Yes — R1, and it does not merely tune selectivity, it reverses it. The same pyrazole, with the same C3 and C5 groups, is a COX-2 drug with a sulfonamide at R1 and a COX-1 probe with a methoxy.

The first table below isolates it twice. In the 4-chlorophenyl block, all three compounds share the CF₃ and the C5 aryl and differ only in that one para substituent: the sulfonamide (SC-58236) is 0.45 nM at COX-2 and 17.8 µM at COX-1, a 40,000-fold preference; the methoxy (SC-560, the standard COX-1 probe) is 7 nM at COX-1 and 1.03 µM at COX-2, 150-fold the other way; the chloro sits with the methoxy, 55 nM at COX-1 against 4.8 µM at COX-2. One substituent swap moves the isoform ratio by about six orders of magnitude. The 4-methoxyphenyl block repeats the pattern at lower potency — sulfonamide 7.9 nM COX-2 / 2.6 µM COX-1, chloro 750 nM / 18 nM, methoxy 750 nM / 5 nM — which is what makes it a result rather than a coincidence of two compounds.

The second table generalises it over all 145 compounds of the series measured against both isoforms. Ranked by the ratio of the group's medians: sulfonamide 128-fold toward COX-2 (95 compounds), methylsulfone 76-fold (8), methyl 33-fold (11), chloro 26-fold (8), hydrogen 19-fold (13) — and methoxy 29-fold toward COX-1 (5 compounds). Note what the middle of that list means: even a hydrogen at R1 leaves the series 19-fold COX-2-preferring, because the rest of the molecule is already a COX-2 shape. The sulfonamide adds roughly another sevenfold on medians, and far more at the top of the series; the methoxy removes it all and then some.

The accepted structural reason is the side pocket COX-2 has and COX-1 does not — COX-2 carries a valine at position 523 where COX-1 has the bulkier isoleucine, leaving room for the sulfonamide or sulfone to reach a polar niche. The data here cannot prove that; what it shows is that the group at R1 is where the isoform choice is made.

Two honest qualifications.

  • The sulfonamide does not have to be at R1. Two compounds in the set carry it on the C5 aryl instead, with a methoxy or fluorine on the N1 aryl, and are still 6.7 and 10 nM at COX-2. The one of those with a COX-1 value is 80 nM there — eightfold selective, not thousandfold. Selectivity belongs to the whole arrangement, not to the presence of a sulfonamide somewhere.
  • R2 and R3 tune potency inside an isoform, not the isoform. Among the 95 sulfonamides measured against both, R3 and R2 move COX-2 from 0.45 nM to above 1 µM, while 93 of the 95 COX-1 values sit between 1 µM and 100 µM (quartiles 10, 29 and 51 µM). The C5 aryl is a potency knob, not a selectivity one. The two exceptions are celecoxib's 13 nM, which the next answer takes apart, and one 830 nM record.

Where the numbers come from, and what they are not. Each bar in the first table is that compound's single most potent exact IC50 at that isoform, which is what the SAR tools rank on. For compounds measured once or twice — most of this set — that is simply the measurement. For celecoxib, measured 225 times at COX-2 and 95 times at COX-1, the pair of best values (0.52 and 13 nM) is not a selectivity ratio at all, and the 25-fold it implies is the subject of the next answer. The second table uses medians per group for exactly that reason.

How this was answered

  1. decompose_r_groups was run again with the same core against COX-1 (target 7854): 162 of its 1,807 compounds match, and 145 of them are also in the COX-2 set.
  2. Those 145 rows, joined on monomerid, give each compound's COX-2 and COX-1 value; grouping them by their R1 cell gives the medians in the second table.
  3. The blocks in the first table are the rows that share R2 and R3 and differ at R1 — the same thing matched_molecular_pairs finds automatically, here read straight off the decomposition.
  4. draw_r_group_table drew both: compounds in the first, one row per R1 substituent in the second.

👤 User

Celecoxib comes out only 25-fold selective in that table, which is not what the literature says. Check it.

🧠 Assistant

The 25-fold is an artefact of dividing one extreme by another. Celecoxib is BindingDB's most measured COX inhibitor, and the two numbers that ratio came from are the best of each pile:

Entry Target Exact IC50 values Sources Best Median Worst
BDBM11639 ("CLX") COX-2 225 188 0.52 nM 80 nM 430 µM
BDBM11639 ("CLX") COX-1 95 86 13 nM 14 µM 12.5 mM
BDBM91683 ("Celecoxib") COX-2 4 4 45 nM 48.5 nM 63 nM
BDBM91683 ("Celecoxib") COX-1 1 1 8.97 µM — —
BDBM50224109 ("Celecoxib") COX-2 1 1 130 nM — —

Best against best gives 25-fold, medians give 175-fold, and the honest number is neither. The two piles come from different sets of papers, so dividing their medians compares a COX-2 literature with a COX-1 literature. The comparison that means something is within one source: 83 papers measured celecoxib against both isoforms, and their ratios run from 2.5-fold to 3,600-fold with a median of 46-fold — the same order as the tens-of-fold usually quoted from human whole-blood assays. The spread is the assay, not the compound: recombinant enzyme, ovine enzyme, cell lines and whole blood all appear, at different substrate concentrations, and a single paper's pair of numbers is internally consistent in a way that the pooled extremes are not.

Both extremes are single records. The 0.52 nM at COX-2 comes from one 2008 paper (PMID 17714833, via ChEMBL) and the 13 nM at COX-1 from one 2023 paper (PMID 36481449). Of the 225 COX-2 values, 11 are below 10 nM and 24 are above 1 µM; the cluster of early Searle papers that defined the compound reports 2 nM. Nothing is obviously wrong with the 0.52 nM, but a single most-potent record is a weak thing to hang a selectivity claim on, and the SAR tools pick exactly that record by design: one row per compound at its most potent exact measurement is the right convention for "how potent can this series get" and the wrong one for "how do these two numbers compare".

Two curation notes fell out of the check.

  • Celecoxib is in BindingDB twice: BDBM11639 and BDBM91683 are the same molecule, written with and without explicit bond characters in the source, and they carry separate measurements (225 and 4 at COX-2). Anything that counts compounds — including the 366 of the first answer — counts them as two. exact_structure_search is what finds all entries of one molecule.
  • BDBM50224109 is also filed as "Celecoxib" and is not celecoxib: it is the methylsulfone analogue, the sulfonamide NH₂ replaced by CH₃. Its single 130 nM value is in the methylsulfone group of the second answer, where it belongs; the name is wrong, the structure is right, and the structure is what the tools decompose.

None of this touches the previous answer. The isoform switch at R1 was read from compounds measured once or twice each in the same papers — SC-58236 and SC-560 differ by 40,000-fold and 150-fold in opposite directions, far beyond what assay scatter produces — and the group medians were taken over 145 compounds, not over celecoxib's 320 records. What the check changes is the one row in that table where the evidence is thickest: celecoxib's own selectivity is best quoted as about 46-fold, from the 83 papers that measured both.

How this was answered

  1. A run_sql query over activity for the three monomerids filed under celecoxib's name, grouped by target, gave the counts, source counts and the best, median and worst value in the table.
  2. A second query joined celecoxib's COX-2 and COX-1 values by source_id, giving the 83 papers that report both and the distribution of their ratios.
  3. exact_structure_search on celecoxib's SMILES returned exactly two entries, BDBM11639 and BDBM91683, as the same molecule; compound.smiles showed the third is the methylsulfone.
