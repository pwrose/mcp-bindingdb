HIV-1 protease inhibitors with PDB structures. BindingDB splits the protease across many target records (different constructs and strains), so the answer depends on which record you look at. The richest is the Q7K/L33I/C67A/C95A construct (complex 125), which has 13 compounds with linked crystal structures, all with Ki values:

Other records add more: the A-series symmetric inhibitors A-77003 (Ki 0.012 nM, 1HVI/1HVS), A-76928 (0.011 nM, 1HVK) and A-74704 (4.5 nM, 9HVP) on complex 50, plus lopinavir (6PJB) and the cyclic urea DMP323 (Ki 0.27 nM, 3JVW) on complex 56. Those come from different protease records, so their Ki values aren't strictly comparable to the table above.

The overlay shows the four most potent from the consistent record — nelfinavir, TMC-126, darunavir and ritonavir — superposed on 3NDX (ritonavir, 1.03 Å). The fits are tight: pocket RMSDs of 0.34–0.39 Å with no binding-site residue displaced more than 2 Å, so all four bind an essentially identical pocket conformation. What differs is the hydrogen bonding:

  • All four hydrogen-bond to the catalytic aspartates D25:A and D25:B — the transition-state hydroxyl engaging the catalytic dyad, the shared anchor of every peptidomimetic in this class.
  • Darunavir and TMC-126 are the most extensively hydrogen-bonded: both add D29:B, D30:A, D30:B and G27:B. Those bis-THF/methoxy-benzoxazole contacts into the backbone of the S2 subsite are exactly the design feature that makes darunavir hold up against resistance mutations — it grips main-chain atoms that can't mutate away.
  • Ritonavir instead reaches up to G48:B in the flap, along with D29:B and G27:B — a flap contact the others don't make.
  • Nelfinavir is the sparsest, only D25:A/B plus D30:A, consistent with its smaller, less peptide-like scaffold.

One caveat worth flagging: several of these Ki values rest on a single measurement (nelfinavir's 0.01 nM, ritonavir's 0.02 nM), and 0.01 nM is below what most protease assays can resolve. The ranking among the top four is within assay noise — treat them as "all sub-0.1 nM" rather than genuinely ordered.
