👤 User

How many measurements are censored ('>' or '<'), broken down by affinity type? Why does that matter?

🧠 Assistant

About one in five BindingDB measurements is censored: 427,470 are reported as "greater than" and 227,013 as "less than", out of 3,248,380. All other measurements are exact ('='). No other relation symbols occur.

Affinity type Measurements '>' '<' % censored
IC50 2,220,594 281,988 188,521 21.2
Ki 623,778 62,249 18,766 13.0
EC50 270,430 42,889 11,042 19.9
Kd 131,550 40,336 8,682 37.3
koff 1,022 6 2 0.8
kon 1,006 2 0 0.2

A '>' value (such as "> 10,000 nM") means the compound did not reach 50% effect at the highest concentration tested. In practice it is a measured non-binder. A '<' value means the compound was more potent than the assay could resolve. Kd has the highest censoring rate (37%), which is typical of large kinase-profiling panels where most compound-kinase pairs show no binding.

Why it matters: an example. Imatinib (BDBM13530) has 25 measurements on wild-type human EGFR:

Summary Result
All 25 values 21 are censored as inactive (20 × "> 10000", 1 × "> 100000"); median pAffinity 5.0
Only the 4 exact values 0.11 nM, 7,600 nM (three times); median pAffinity 5.12
Single best value pAffinity 9.96 (0.11 nM)

Three common mistakes follow from this:

  1. Dropping censored values (WHERE relation = '=') throws away 21 of 25 data points, including all the evidence that imatinib does not bind EGFR. The remaining 4 values give the 0.11 nM outlier a quarter of the weight.
  2. Treating bounds as exact values. The stored p_affinity for "> 10000 nM" is 5.0, the bound itself. The true value is 5.0 or lower, so an average that includes it overstates potency. A median is more robust, which is why it works here.
  3. Ranking by the best value. The maximum (9.96) suggests sub-nanomolar EGFR binding, while 21 of the 25 measurements say the opposite.

In the BindingDB tools, the max_value_nm filter (for example in find_ligands_for_target) excludes '>' values automatically. Keep that in mind when you use it to count compounds that bind.

How this was answered

  1. run_sql counts measurements per affinity_type with FILTER (WHERE relation = ...).
  2. run_sql checks which relation symbols exist at all, to make sure nothing is hiding outside '=', '>' and '<'.
  3. run_sql compares summaries with and without censored values for imatinib on EGFR, using list(raw_value ...) to show every original value.
