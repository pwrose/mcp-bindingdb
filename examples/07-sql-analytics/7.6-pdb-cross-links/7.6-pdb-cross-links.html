Lessons about cross-references. This query looks simple but needs four things to come out right:

  1. The target has many names. HIV-1 protease is a homodimer cut from the Gag-Pol polyprotein. BindingDB records it as "HIV-1 protease", "Protease", "Gag-Pol polyprotein [489-587]", "Dimer of Gag-Pol polyprotein [501-599]" and dozens of mutant constructs, as both single proteins and complexes. The query uses a regular expression for these names. Plain "Gag-Pol polyprotein" records are left out, because the same name is also used for reverse transcriptase.
  2. PDB links sit on one compound record. Amprenavir's 58 protease measurements are on BDBM577, but its PDB ids (ligand code 478) are attached to a different record, BDBM50163391. Joining on monomerid finds nothing. Matching on the first 14 characters of the InChIKey (the connectivity block) connects the two records.
  3. That match ignores stereochemistry. The first InChIKey block is the same for all stereoisomers. Several stereoisomers of A-76928 (BDBM197 = A-76889, BDBM199 = A-77003, BDBM50041035 and others) are all assigned the same three A79 structures, although only one of them is in the crystal. The 91 count is therefore an upper bound, and the list above keeps only the most potent stereoisomer of each structure set.
  4. Coverage is incomplete. Indinavir (BDBM517) and several other approved protease inhibitors have many PDB structures, but BindingDB carries no PDB link for them. A search for their ligand codes (MK1 indinavir, 017 darunavir, ROC saquinavir, RIT ritonavir) finds nothing. The 91 count is therefore also a lower bound on what the PDB actually holds. To check a specific compound, search the PDB by its InChIKey or ligand code.

How this was answered

  1. run_sql on target finds the HIV-1 protease records, both single proteins and complexes.
  2. run_sql checks two approved drugs (amprenavir and indinavir): the records that hold their measurements carry no PDB links.
  3. run_sql searches monomer.het_pdb for known protease-inhibitor ligand codes. Only amprenavir's 478 is present, on BDBM50163391.
  4. run_sql shows that the InChIKey connectivity block links BDBM577 to the record that holds the PDB ids.
  5. run_sql combines it all: the protease ligands, their PDB ids (pooled by InChIKey block), and the PDB ids of the protease protein records (polymer.pdb_ids). It counts the overlap, then lists the top compounds.

The comma-separated id lists are split with unnest(string_split(...)).
