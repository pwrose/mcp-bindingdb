Which compounds measured against HIV-1 protease also have a crystal structure in the PDB?

🧠 Assistant

7,055 compounds have a measurement on an HIV-1 protease record in BindingDB. 94 of them are linked to 106 PDB entries in which the ligand is bound to HIV-1 protease. The grid below shows the ones with the most structures (three or more), plus the two most potent of those with two. Each card gives the PDB ligand code and entries and a bar for the compound's Ki against the protease.

Amprenavir (BDBM577, ligand code 478) leads by far, with 20 protease structures (1HPV, 1T7J, 3EKP, 3EKV, 3EM3, 3NU3, 3NU4, 3NU5, 3NU6, 3NU9, 3NUJ, 3NUO, 3OXV, 3S43, 3S45, 4JEC, 4RVJ, 5KR0, 5T8H, 7N6V) and 58 protease measurements. TMC-126 (BDBM9236) has five structures, and five compounds have three each: A-76928 (BDBM198), the reduced-amide peptide BDBM17350, and three bis-THF sulfonamides from one series, CHEMBL4459766, CHEMBL4563518 and CHEMBL4521688. The last two cards, CHEMBL3605643 and CHEMBL3577575, have two structures each and the tightest Ki values in the set (1.5 pM and 6 pM), each from one or two measurements. Seven of the nine compounds share the hydroxyethylamine sulfonamide core of amprenavir, highlighted in the grid.

This is a good starting set if you want to relate measured affinity to binding mode, because each compound has both potency data and a structure of its complex with the protease.

Lessons about cross-references. This query looks simple but needs four things to come out right:

  1. The target has many names. HIV-1 protease is a homodimer cut from the Gag-Pol polyprotein. BindingDB records it as "HIV-1 protease", "Protease", "Gag-Pol polyprotein [489-587]", "Dimer of Gag-Pol polyprotein [501-599]" and dozens of mutant constructs, as both single proteins and complexes. The query uses a regular expression for these names. Plain "Gag-Pol polyprotein" records are left out, because the same name is also used for reverse transcriptase.
  2. PDB links sit on one compound record. Amprenavir's 58 protease measurements are on BDBM577, but its PDB ids (ligand code 478) are attached to a different record, BDBM50163391. Joining on monomerid finds nothing. Matching on the first 14 characters of the InChIKey (the connectivity block) connects the two records.
  3. That match ignores stereochemistry. The first InChIKey block is the same for all stereoisomers. Several stereoisomers of A-76928 (BDBM197 = A-76889, BDBM199 = A-77003, BDBM50041035 and others) are all assigned the same three A79 structures, although only one of them is in the crystal. The 94 count is therefore an upper bound, and the grid keeps only the most potent stereoisomer of each structure set.
  4. Coverage is incomplete. Indinavir (BDBM517) and several other approved protease inhibitors have many PDB structures, but BindingDB carries no PDB link for them. A search for their ligand codes (MK1 indinavir, 017 darunavir, ROC saquinavir, RIT ritonavir) finds nothing. The 94 count is therefore also a lower bound on what the PDB actually holds. To check a specific compound, search the PDB by its InChIKey or ligand code.

The counts also depend on which constructs you call "HIV-1 protease": the target names were matched with

regexp_matches(name, '^(Dimer of )?Gag-Pol polyprotein \[(4[89]\d|5[01]\d)-(5[89]\d|6[01]\d)[,\]]')
OR ((name ILIKE 'HIV-1 protease%' OR name ILIKE 'Protease')
    AND (organism ILIKE '%immunodeficiency virus%' OR organism IS NULL))

which keeps the protease fragments (residues about 482–612 of Gag-Pol) and drops reverse transcriptase and integrase fragments. A narrower pattern drops a few mutant constructs and with them a few PDB entries.

How this was answered

  1. run_sql on target finds the HIV-1 protease records, both single proteins and complexes, with the pattern above.
  2. run_sql checks two approved drugs (amprenavir and indinavir): the records that hold their measurements carry no PDB links.
  3. run_sql searches monomer.het_pdb for known protease-inhibitor ligand codes. Only amprenavir's 478 is present, on BDBM50163391.
  4. run_sql shows that the InChIKey connectivity block links BDBM577 to the record that holds the PDB ids.
  5. run_sql combines it all: the protease ligands, their PDB ids (pooled by InChIKey block), and the PDB ids of the protease protein records (polymer.pdb_ids). It counts the overlap, then lists the top compounds with their Ki values.
  6. The comma-separated id lists are split with unnest(string_split(...)).
  7. draw_structure_grid: the nine compounds with their PDB ids as subtitles and a protease Ki bar each, aligned on the hydroxyethylamine sulfonamide core; compounds with fewer than three Ki values are drawn faint.
