The name search also returned two other records with the same InChIKey or a close variant: BDBM31341 (an imatinib entry with no measurements attached) and BDBM50434581 (imatinib-CD3, a deuterated version). A single drug can appear under several BindingDB ids, so check the measurement counts and use the id that carries the data.

Main targets. These are the known clinical targets of imatinib. The table shows the median across all measurements for each human target, next to the single best value:

(pAffinity = -log10 of the affinity in molar units, so 9 = 1 nM, 7 = 100 nM, 5 = 10 uM.)

A lesson about "best" values. The compound summary ranks targets by their single most potent measurement. On that ranking imatinib's top two targets are ERBB2 (best pAffinity 10.2) and EGFR (9.96), which is misleading. For EGFR, 21 of the 25 measurements are reported as "> 10 uM" (inactive), and the median is 5.0. The sub-nanomolar value is a single outlier. When you judge how strongly a compound binds a target, look at the median and the number of measurements, not only the best value.

How this was answered

  1. search_compounds with the query "imatinib": finds compounds by name, synonym or identifier. Exact name matches rank first.
  2. get_compound with id 13530: returns identifiers, synonyms and a per-target summary of the most potent measurements.
  3. run_sql: computes the median affinity per target from the activity table, which holds one row per measurement.
