02Method — queries as run

Filter: affinity type in {Ki, Kd}, value ≤ 10 nM, relation not >. IC50 and EC50 were deliberately excluded, as the prompt asks for binding constants.

1. Count all 4-anilinoquinazoline compounds in BindingDB

substructure_search(query="c1ccc(cc1)Nc1ncnc2ccccc12", count_only=true)

6,323 matching compounds out of 1,464,874 indexed structures.

2. Group the matching compounds' targets, keeping only Ki/Kd ≤ 10 nM

substructure_search(query="c1ccc(cc1)Nc1ncnc2ccccc12", summarize_by_target=true,
                    affinity_types=["Ki","Kd"], max_value_nm=10, limit=500)

3. Per-target ligand lists (16 calls, one per target)

06What the result set shows

  • The chemotype is overwhelmingly an ATP‑site kinase scaffold. 12 of 16 targets and 34 of 38 class‑level ligand slots are protein kinases; the quinazoline N1/N3 pair hydrogen‑bonds to the hinge backbone and the 4‑anilino group occupies the hydrophobic back pocket.
  • ErbB dominates, as expected for the scaffold's drug history. Five approved drugs appear in the set — gefitinib, erlotinib, lapatinib, afatinib and vandetanib — plus the clinical candidates canertinib and CP‑724714 and the tool compound PD153035 (Ki = 6 pM, the most potent measurement in the whole result set).
  • Several EGFR “ligands” are PROTACs and molecular glues, not small-molecule inhibitors: six of the 20 EGFR hits (MW 900–1,150) are gefitinib- or lapatinib-like warheads linked to thalidomide or VHL ligands. They qualify on Kd but are a different modality.
  • Off‑target classes are real pharmacology. GAK (a NAK‑family kinase) is a well‑documented erlotinib/gefitinib off‑target, RIPK2 has its own dedicated anilinoquinazoline series, and the DYRK1A/CLK1 hit is a deliberately designed thiazole‑fused variant.
  • The GPCR class is a genuine scaffold re‑use. The histamine H3 and TAAR1 ligands carry basic amine side chains on the aniline ring and reach sub‑nanomolar Ki without any kinase intent — the quinazoline here acts as a lipophilic aromatic anchor, not a hinge binder.
  • One class is a database artifact. The HIV‑1 protease entry is a compound‑to‑citation mismatch (see the caveat in panel 6); excluding it leaves five pharmacologically meaningful classes.

07Limitations

  • Counting is at the level of BindingDB target records, which treat point mutants and construct ranges as separate targets (four EGFR records plus one EGFR complex record here). Collapsing them to UniProt accessions gives 11 distinct gene products rather than 16 targets.
  • Duplicate compound records exist: BDBM5447 and BDBM50338602 are both gefitinib, deposited from different sources. They are counted once each by the per‑target tool, so the EGFR ligand count of 20 is 19 unique chemical entities.
  • Two measurements use a < relation (e.g. Kd < 0.2 nM); these are kept as qualifying, while > relations are excluded.
  • Affinities come from heterogeneous assays (competition binding panels, radioligand displacement, fluorescence) and are not strictly comparable across rows.
  • Targets whose only sub‑10 nM evidence is an IC50 are absent by design — the true off‑target breadth of this chemotype is considerably wider.
