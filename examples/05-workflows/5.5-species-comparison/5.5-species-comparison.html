👤 User

For the dopamine D2 receptor, do compounds bind the human and rat receptors with similar affinity?

🧠 Assistant

Mostly yes. Most compounds bind the human and rat receptors within 10-fold of each other, and the antagonist reference drugs agree within 2-fold. About one compound in seven differs by more than 10-fold.

BindingDB has a separate target for each species: human D2 (UniProt P14416, 17,253 measurements on 10,904 compounds) and rat D2 (P61169, 6,974 measurements on 4,936 compounds). The mouse, cow, pig and monkey receptors are also present, with far less data. I paired every compound that has an exact ("=") Ki on both the human and the rat receptor, took the median per species, and compared the two:

Human and rat D2 are orthologs with a conserved binding site, so close agreement is what you would expect. The exception is the agonist quinpirole, which looks about 6-fold more potent at the rat receptor. Agonist Ki values depend on which receptor state the assay measures (high- or low-affinity, G-protein coupled or not), and rat assays often used brain tissue while human data mostly come from cloned receptors in cell lines. A difference like this one more likely reflects the assay than the species.

The lesson: when two values differ, first ask whether the assays differ. Comparing orthologs in BindingDB really compares two sets of experiments: different labs, radioligands, tissues and years. Three checks before you read a gap as biology:

  • Same paper or not. I split the pairs into those where one paper measured both species and those where human and rat values come from different papers. The agreement was about the same (mean absolute difference 0.55 vs 0.51 log units), so the spread is not just lab-to-lab noise.
  • Identical values. 48 of the 375 pairs have exactly the same median. In the one I checked (BDBM50086852), a 1995 rat paper and a 2000 human paper both report 12 nM. The later paper probably quoted the earlier value rather than measuring it again. Before trusting a perfect match, open both records with get_activity or look at the citations.
  • Duplicate compound records. Clozapine appears twice (BDBM50001884 and BDBM22869, drawn as different tautomers). For a complete picture, group by the InChIKey first block rather than by BindingDB id.

How this was answered

  1. search_targets with the query "DRD2": lists the D2 receptor once per species, each with its own id (human polymerid 5866, rat 3630) and measurement count.
  2. run_sql: for each compound, species and affinity type, takes the median exact-relation p_affinity from the activity table and joins human to rat on the compound id. The same query computed the summary statistics, the same-paper vs different-paper split and the count of identical pairs.
  3. run_sql again: lists the most heavily measured shared compounds (the reference-drug table) and the raw records behind one identical pair.
  4. matplotlib: draws the scatter plot. add_figure adds it to this transcript.
