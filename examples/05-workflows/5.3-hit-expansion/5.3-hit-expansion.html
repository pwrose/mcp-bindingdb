👤 User

I have a screening hit: Brc1cccc(Nc2ncnc3ccccc23)c1 — a 3-bromoanilino quinazoline. Find analogues, show the best ones, and tell me what the SAR suggests.

🧠 Assistant

The hit itself is already in BindingDB. An exact-structure search matched BDBM3264, measured as EGFR IC50 27 nM in three separate papers (27, 27 and 28 nM; 1995, 1996 and 2008). A hit that is already a known compound with reproduced data is the best possible starting point: the chemistry around it has been done.

The scaffold is enormous. The 4-anilinoquinazoline core (c1ccc(Nc2ncnc3ccccc23)cc1) matches 6,323 compounds, measured against 409 targets. The main ones:

Target Compounds Measurements Best pAffinity
EGFR (P00533) 2,525 4,253 11.52
ERBB2 (P04626) 939 1,245 10.40
VEGFR2 (P35968) 798 928 9.22
MKNK2 (Q9HBH9) 508 808 9.10

So this is the EGFR inhibitor scaffold, and the best compounds on it reach IC50 0.003 nM — about 10,000 times more potent than the hit. Afatinib, an approved drug, sits on the same core (EGFR IC50 0.01 nM, 87 measurements).

The SAR. Rather than taking the most potent compounds from across 6,323 molecules, which would mix assays from hundreds of labs, I pulled the analogues measured in the same two papers as the hit. That gives a clean congeneric series where the values can be compared directly. The figure below shows the hit with seven of them, aligned on the shared core. Three rules come out of it:

  1. Add 6,7-dialkoxy groups to the quinazoline — this is where the potency is. 6,7-dimethoxy (PD153035) takes the hit from 27 nM to 0.025 nM, a 1,000-fold gain; 6,7-diethoxy reaches 0.006 nM. Longer chains then lose it again (dipropoxy 0.17 nM, dibutoxy 105 nM), so there is a pocket with a size limit.
  2. Keep the halogen at the meta position. 3-Br (27 nM) and 3-Cl (23 nM) are equivalent, 3-I is 3x weaker, 3-F is 2x weaker and no substituent is 13x weaker. Moving the same bromine to para costs 40x (0.025 to 0.96 nM) and to ortho costs 5,000x (128 nM).
  3. The anilino NH is essential. Methylating it turns the 0.025 nM compound into a 152 nM one, a 6,000-fold loss. That NH donates a hydrogen bond in the ATP site, and a single methyl group removes it.

Lesson: for SAR, prefer one lab's series over the database's best values. IC50 depends on ATP concentration, enzyme construct and readout, so values from different papers can differ by 10x for the same compound without either being wrong. Searching globally for "the most potent analogues" gives a list spanning dozens of assays, where real SAR and assay differences are impossible to separate. Filtering to a single source, as here, gives smaller numbers but trustworthy ratios. The database's broad view and a single paper's series answer different questions: the first says what is achievable on the scaffold, the second says what each change does.

How this was answered

  1. exact_structure_search with the hit's SMILES: confirmed it is BDBM3264 and already measured.
  2. find_targets_for_compound (best_per_target=false): showed all five of its measurements, which gave the reproduced 27 nM on EGFR.
  3. substructure_search with the core, count_only=true and then summarize_by_target=true: sized the scaffold (6,323 compounds) and found which targets it is used against.
  4. substructure_search with target_id=520 (EGFR) and max_value_nm=10: found the most potent compounds on the scaffold, including afatinib.
  5. run_sql on activity: pulled every EGFR IC50 from the hit's own two source papers, which is the congeneric series the SAR rests on.
  6. draw_structure_grid: drew eight of them aligned on the core, with the core highlighted.
