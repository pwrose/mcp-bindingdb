👤 User

Which well-known drugs bind the hERG potassium channel (KCNH2) below 1 µM?

🧠 Assistant

First, a limitation to state up front: BindingDB has no "approved drug" flag. It records measurements, not regulatory status. So there is no query that returns "approved drugs". What I did instead was check a list of 30 drugs known for cardiac or QT-interval issues against BindingDB by name. A systematic answer would need an external source of approved drugs (DrugBank, ChEMBL's max_phase) joined on InChIKey.

The scale of the hERG data. Human KCNH2 (UniProt Q12809) has 18,767 measurements on 14,872 compounds from 2,018 sources — one of the most-screened targets in the database, because every drug programme tests for it.

The drugs that bind below 1 µM. 19 of the 30 drugs have a median below 1 µM on human wild-type KCNH2. The grid below shows the 12 most potent, from astemizole (5 nM) to domperidone (162 nM), with each drug's therapeutic class. Every bar is the median of that drug's exact IC50 values, the one measurement type all of them have; the note gives the number of values and the best one. The remaining drugs, by their median over all exact Ki, Kd and IC50 values:

This list contains two very different things. Astemizole, terfenadine and cisapride were withdrawn or restricted for QT prolongation and torsades de pointes, and their hERG affinity is the reason. But dofetilide and ibutilide are antiarrhythmics whose therapeutic mechanism IS blocking this channel — potent hERG binding is what they are for. The antipsychotics (haloperidol, droperidol, pimozide, sertindole, thioridazine, ziprasidone) carry QT warnings and remain in use with monitoring.

Lesson: affinity alone does not tell you the risk, and the median matters. What determines cardiac risk is the margin between the hERG IC50 and the free drug concentration reached in plasma, not the IC50 by itself. Terfenadine was withdrawn while fexofenadine, its active metabolite and a far weaker blocker (median 23 µM), replaced it. Note also how far the best value sits from the median: terfenadine's best IC50 is 8.9 nM against a median of 130 nM, and amiodarone ranges from 30 nM to 10 µM across five measurements. The measurement type moves the numbers too: clozapine's nine IC50s have a median of 324 nM, its four Ki values a median of 10.5 µM. hERG IC50 depends strongly on the assay (patch clamp versus binding displacement, temperature, pulse protocol), so with 2,018 different sources the spread is wide. Quoting a single best value from the literature would move several of these drugs by more than tenfold.

How this was answered

  • search_targets for KCNH2 (Homo sapiens): found human Q12809 (polymerid 2132).
  • run_sql on activity: counted the measurements, compounds and sources for that target.
  • run_sql joining compound_name to a hand-written list of 30 drug names: matched each name to its BindingDB records and took, per drug, the median and best of the exact values on human wild-type KCNH2 (uniprot_raw = uniprot), over all Ki, Kd and IC50 values and over IC50 alone, with the count of measurements.
  • run_sql on compound for the structures of the 12 most potent drugs, then draw_structure_grid with one card per drug and its IC50 median as the bar.
