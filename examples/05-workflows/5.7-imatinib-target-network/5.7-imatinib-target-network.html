Imatinib's targets at ≤ 1 µM

BindingDB has 51 imatinib targets at this threshold. Many are the same protein several times: kinase-domain constructs and resistance mutants of ABL1 and KIT are separate entries. Folding those into their UniProt entry and keeping human proteins leaves 30 targets:

The classic targets are all there: ABL1 (Kd 1 nM; the BCR-ABL1 complex is IC50 1.1 nM), KIT, PDGFRA/B, DDR1/2 and CSF1R. Left out of the 30 are three complex targets (BCR-ABL1, ABL1/ABL2, PDGFRA/B) and mouse and rat orthologues.

* Treat the ERBB2 and EGFR values as suspect. Both come from one 2022 paper (doi 10.1016/j.ejmech.2022.114502), each from a single measurement. Imatinib is known not to inhibit the EGFR family, and these would make them its most potent targets. All eight carbonic anhydrase values also come from one 2009 study (doi 10.1016/j.bmcl.2009.06.002). The transporter values are IC50s for inhibiting transport, not binding constants.

The network

BindingDB has no protein–protein interaction data, so a physical interaction network can't be built from it. What BindingDB can show at the same threshold is a shared-ligand network: two targets are linked when other compounds (imatinib excluded) bind both at ≤ 1 µM. This is a pharmacological map of which of imatinib's targets are hit by the same chemistry, not a map of proteins binding each other. I counted distinct compounds by InChIKey and drew an edge for pairs that share at least 10 (the figure below).

The targets fall into separate groups with almost no chemistry crossing between them:

  • Kinases form one dense group: 116 of the 136 possible pairs share at least 10 potent ligands. The strongest links follow kinase families: EGFR–ERBB2 (1,547 shared compounds), KIT–FLT3 (543), DDR1–DDR2 (411), KIT–PDGFRA (347), PDGFRA–PDGFRB (338) and LCK–LYN (165). ABL1, the target imatinib is used against, is linked to all 16 other kinases but never strongly: its largest overlap is with KIT (161). PIP4K2C, a lipid kinase, hangs on by two weak links (ABL1 11, EGFR 10).
  • Carbonic anhydrases form a complete group of their own: all 28 pairs are linked, and CA1–CA2 share 4,986 compounds, the strongest link in the network. No kinase shares more than 5 potent ligands with any carbonic anhydrase.
  • ABCB1 and ABCG2, the two efflux pumps, share 25 inhibitors with each other and almost none with anything else.
  • MATE1, MATE2 and NQO2 are isolated. The two MATE transporters share 3 ligands (BindingDB has only 8 and 4 potent ones for them), and NQO2 shares at most 4 with any target.

So imatinib's ≤ 1 µM profile spans chemistries that other compounds keep apart: kinase inhibitors, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors and transporter inhibitors are, in BindingDB, almost entirely different sets of compounds.

Reading the edges. Edge counts favour well-studied targets: EGFR has 10,921 potent ligands in BindingDB and HIPK4 only 37, so a count of 20 means much more for HIPK4. Kinase-panel datasets, where one compound is tested on hundreds of kinases, probably add many of the weak kinase–kinase links, which would help explain why the kinase group is so dense.
