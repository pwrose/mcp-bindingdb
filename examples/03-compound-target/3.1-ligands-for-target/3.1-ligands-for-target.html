What are the 20 most potent EGFR inhibitors in BindingDB with a Ki or Kd below 1 nM?

🧠 Assistant

BindingDB has 14,126 compounds measured against human EGFR (wild type, UniProt P00533; BindingDB target id 520). Restricting to Ki or Kd ≤ 1 nM still leaves more than 20 compounds. These are the 12 most potent, ranked by each compound's single best Ki/Kd. The last two columns summarize all of the compound's Ki/Kd values on EGFR.

Rows 13–20 of the result include gefitinib (Iressa, best Ki 0.4 nM, median 9.0 over 50 Ki/Kd values) and several single-measurement compounds from medicinal-chemistry papers.

Most of the list is the 4-anilinoquinazoline chemotype: PD153035, gefitinib, erlotinib, canertinib and afatinib. Several compounds also carry an acrylamide (C=CC(=O)N), the warhead of covalent EGFR inhibitors.

Lesson: a top-N list ranks single best values. The tool ranks each compound by its one most potent measurement, so a compound measured once in one paper can top a drug measured 40 times. PD153035 is first because of a single Ki of 6 pM. The tool also returns relations: CHEMBL5394686 is listed as "< 0.2 nM", which is an upper bound, not an exact value. Two compounds show how much a single value can overstate potency. Staurosporine has a best Kd of 0.35 nM, but its median over 25 Ki/Kd values is pAffinity 6.6 (~250 nM). CHEMBL1229592 has a best of 0.3 nM and a median of 7.9 (~13 nM). Read the number of measurements and the median before you believe a ranking.

How this was answered

  1. search_targets with query "EGFR", organism "Homo sapiens": found wild-type EGFR (target id 520, 14,126 compounds). It also listed mutant and truncated EGFR constructs as separate targets (see Example 3.3).
  2. find_ligands_for_target with target_id 520, affinity_types [Ki, Kd], max_value_nm 1, limit 20: the most potent compounds, one row per compound.
  3. run_sql on the activity and compound tables: added SMILES, the number of Ki/Kd measurements and the median pAffinity for each compound.
