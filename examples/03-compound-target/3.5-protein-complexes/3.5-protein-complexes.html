👤 User

The benzodiazepine binding site sits between two subunits of the GABA-A receptor. How does BindingDB store a target like that, and how selective are the benzodiazepine-site drugs across the subtypes?

🧠 Assistant

BindingDB stores it as a complex target: a target made of several protein components, with its own id and no UniProt accession of its own. The GABA-A receptor that diazepam binds is Gamma-aminobutyric acid receptor subunit alpha-1/beta-3/gamma-2, complex id 386, assembled from three separate polymer entries:

Component UniProt Polymer id
GABA-A receptor subunit α1 P14867 5521
GABA-A receptor subunit β3 P28472 50001801
GABA-A receptor subunit γ2 P18507 50000214

It holds 1,325 measurements on 1,111 compounds (1,258 Ki, 55 EC50, 12 IC50) and links 152 PDB entries. This is the right unit for this question: the benzodiazepine site is a pocket at the interface between the α and the γ2 subunit, so neither protein has the site on its own, and an affinity measured against "GABRA1" alone would not describe it.

Each subunit combination is its own target. 82 GABA-A complex entries cover 2,396 compounds in 7,237 measurements. The five that matter for benzodiazepine-site pharmacology are the αxβ3γ2 receptors:

Subtype selectivity of the benzodiazepine-site drugs

Figure 1 draws each drug with its median Ki against the five receptors, and the four classic ligands fall into three distinct patterns.

  • Diazepam binds all four benzodiazepine-sensitive subtypes about equally — 11 to 24 nM across α1, α2, α3 and α5. Its sedative, anxiolytic, myorelaxant and amnesic effects come from the same molecule hitting all of them, which is the pharmacological basis for the search for subtype-selective successors.
  • Zolpidem is the α1-preferring hypnotic, and the data reproduce the textbook profile exactly: Ki 27 nM at α1, 156 nM at α2, 383 nM at α3, and no measurable binding at α5 or α6 (six records at α5, all reported as Ki > 3.3 µM or higher, and three at α6 reported as > 10 µM). That is why it has no bars for those two.
  • Flumazenil, the overdose antidote, binds everything that has a benzodiazepine site — 0.6 to 1.1 nM at α1, α2, α3 and α5 — and then drops 200-fold to 148 nM at α6. The α6 subunit carries an arginine where the other α subunits carry a histidine, and that one residue is what makes α6 receptors benzodiazepine-insensitive. The complex data show the structural fact directly.
  • Bretazenil is the exception that proves it. This partial agonist binds α1, α2, α3 and α5 sub-nanomolar and keeps 13 nM affinity at α6, 11-fold weaker instead of 200-fold. It is the probe compound used precisely because it tolerates the arginine.

What the subtype split is for. Ranking compounds by α1/α5 ratio finds how far selectivity has been pushed: CHEMBL1081861, an imidazo-triazolo-benzodiazepine from PMID 19740657, reaches Ki 0.9 nM at α5 with 469 nM at α1 — 521-fold — and a 2025 patent series (US20250136581) of triazolylmethoxy-pyridazines reaches 456-fold on the same comparison. α5-selective negative modulators are pursued for cognitive impairment, where diazepam's α5 activity is the liability rather than the goal. None of this is visible if a database stores one "GABA-A receptor" target.

Querying a complex. Every curated tool takes target_kind: "complex" with the complex id: get_target for the record above, find_ligands_for_target for its ligands. The most potent compound on α5β3γ2 is a pyrazoloquinolinone with Ki 0.08 nM (PMID 10633039). In SQL the equivalent is activity.complexid instead of activity.polymerid — the two columns are mutually exclusive, and complex_component maps a complex to its polymers.

Lesson: "complex" means more than one component, not necessarily an assembly. The same target kind also stores fusion proteins. BCR-ABL, for example, is complex 261 with components BCR (P11274) and ABL1 (P00519) — in reality a single polypeptide from a chromosomal translocation, not two proteins in a complex. And an obligate heterodimer like CDK4/cyclin D1 (complex 93, 4,278 compounds) sits in the same table as a receptor with five subunits. Read the component_count and the component list before deciding what a complex target physically is.

Lesson: the same pharmacology is split between polymer and complex entries. The α1 subunit also exists as a standalone polymer target (5521) with 540 measurements on 447 compounds, most of them electrophysiology recordings where the paper named only the α subunit. A survey of "what binds GABA-A receptors" has to collect both kinds of entry, or it will silently miss one of them.

How this was answered

  1. search_targets for "GABA-A receptor" returned both kinds of entry side by side — polymer subunits and complex receptors — each row labelled with its target_kind.
  2. get_target with target_id: 386 and target_kind: "complex" gave the components, the measurement counts by type and the PDB links.
  3. A run_sql query over complex joined to activity listed the GABA-A complexes by size.
  4. A second query took each drug's median exact Ki and its censored ('>') records on the five αxβ3γ2 complexes.
  5. A third query ranked compounds by the ratio of their α1 to their α5 Ki.
  6. find_ligands_for_target with target_kind: "complex" listed the most potent α5β3γ2 ligands, and draw_structure_grid drew Figure 1.
