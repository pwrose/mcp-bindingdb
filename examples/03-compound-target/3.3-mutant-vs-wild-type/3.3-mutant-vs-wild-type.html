Compare inhibitors of EGFR wild type with the T790M mutant.

🧠 Assistant

In BindingDB, EGFR mutants are separate targets that share the UniProt accession P00533 and carry the mutation in brackets. Searching for "EGFR" in human returns wild-type EGFR plus more than a dozen variants. The two compared here:

Target id Target Compounds Measurements
520 EGFR (wild type) 14,126 23,266
243 EGFR [T790M] 213 229

Other variants hold more data than T790M alone. The double mutant [T790M, L858R] (target 60) has 1,276 compounds, and [L858R, T790M, C797S] (target 1058) has 666. T790M is the "gatekeeper" resistance mutation that lets lung tumours escape first-generation drugs such as gefitinib and erlotinib.

A fair comparison. I kept only exact ("=") Ki, Kd or IC50 values and compared each compound's wild-type and T790M values within the same source (same paper or patent, same affinity type). This gives 61 compounds (64 compound–source pairs). All 64 pairs turn out to be IC50 values from US patents; no paper or patent reports a Ki or Kd on both targets. Of these, 26 pairs are at least 10-fold more potent on T790M, 4 are at least 10-fold more potent on wild type, and the median shift is +0.4 log units toward the mutant.

The grid below shows nine of these pairs: each compound with one bar for its wild-type IC50 and one for its T790M IC50 from the same patent, and the fold change under the structure. The top four are mutant-selective, the bottom five lose potency on the mutant. Most bars are faint because each rests on one or two measurements; only rociletinib has four values per target.

Two chemotypes separate cleanly. The 2,4-dianilinopyrimidines (rociletinib and two of its acrylamide analogues from patent US10300058B2, and a 5-chloropyrimidine from US10059688B2) are designed to be mutant-selective, and they are 15- to 150-fold more potent on T790M. The 4-anilinoquinazolines (gefitinib and the acrylamide-bearing quinazolines of US9556191B2) lose potency on the mutant, by 2.6- to 135-fold. That is the drug-resistance mechanism, visible directly in the data. The fourth pair that is at least 10-fold wild-type selective is not drawn: an aminopyrazole from US9416123B2 (BDBM50379214), with a median IC50 of 110 nM on wild type and about 2.6 µM on T790M.

Lesson: compare like with like. Gefitinib appears twice in the grid, and the two patents disagree: 135-fold weaker on T790M in one, only 3.5-fold weaker in the other. Assay format, ATP concentration and enzyme construct all shift IC50 values. Comparing a wild-type value from one paper with a mutant value from another can produce or hide a selectivity window. Pair measurements by source, as done here, before drawing conclusions, and look at the individual values: rociletinib's four values per target in US10300058B2 include one far outlier on each (153,000 nM on wild type, 2,500 nM on T790M), which the median absorbs but a mean would not. Also note that find_ligands_for_target with include_variants=true pools all EGFR variants into one ranked list. That is useful for a quick overview, but to compare variants, query each variant's target id separately.

How this was answered

  • search_targets with query "EGFR", organism "Homo sapiens": listed wild-type EGFR (id 520) and its mutant and truncated constructs, including T790M (id 243).
  • find_ligands_for_target with uniprot P00533, include_variants true: a pooled ranking across all EGFR variants. Wild-type measurements and recent patent data on other mutant constructs fill the top of the list; T790M alone (target 243) does not appear in the top 20.
  • run_sql on the activity table: paired each compound's median pAffinity on targets 520 and 243 within the same source and affinity type, counted the pairs and fold changes, then joined to compound for names and structures.
  • draw_structure_grid: nine compound–patent pairs, each with a wild-type and a T790M IC50 bar, aligned on the 4-anilinopyrimidine shared by both chemotypes.
