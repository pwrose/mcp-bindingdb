👤 User

For BACE1, how has ligand efficiency changed over the years? Rank the most efficient inhibitors, and plot potency against size per period.

🧠 Assistant

BACE1 inhibitors became both smaller and more potent. Between the first period and the most recent one the median compound lost about 17 heavy atoms while gaining a log unit of potency, so the median ligand efficiency rose from 0.18 to 0.33 kcal/mol per heavy atom. Figure 1 shows the shift period by period.

Ligand efficiency over time

First published Compounds Median heavy atoms Median pAffinity Median LE LE ≥ 0.3
2001–2004 231 50 6.40 0.18 1%
2005–2009 918 39 6.68 0.24 7%
2010–2014 2,669 31 6.74 0.29 47%
2015–2019 5,475 31 7.38 0.33 70%
2020–2026 1,011 33 7.00 0.30 52%

LE = 1.37 × pAffinity / heavy atoms. A compound is counted once, at its best exact ('=') Ki, Kd or IC50 against human BACE1 (target 5093), in the period of its first measurement. 1.37 kcal/mol is the free energy of one log unit of affinity at 298 K, so LE is roughly how much binding energy each heavy atom earns.

The turn came between 2009 and 2014. The early compounds are peptidic transition-state mimetics of the amyloid precursor protein: they bind the two catalytic aspartates with a hydroxyethylamine or statine group, and they are enormous, with a median of 50 heavy atoms. The leap in the 2010–2014 period is the arrival of the cyclic amidine series — aminothiazines, aminohydantoins and the aminooxazines — which pick up the same aspartate pair with a small basic ring, and the whole field converged on them.

The top two are not inhibitors in any practical sense. The first is a 4-heavy-atom fragment recorded as a stand-in for the hydroxyethylamine warhead; because LE divides by heavy atoms, anything that tiny with a measurable value wins. Ligand efficiency is only comparable within a size range: pass properties: {min_heavy_atoms: 20} and the ranking becomes the one above from the third row down, which is a real series.

The record holder is a patent series. US9273042B2 (compound 6, IC50 0.2 pM) tops the list on both counts. Patent values at the picomolar end usually come from a different assay format than the biochemical IC50s most papers report, so treat the absolute number with care — but the series is genuinely efficient, at 0.58 kcal/mol per heavy atom.

Lipophilic efficiency ranks something else entirely. LipE = pAffinity − cLogP, which rewards potency that does not come from greasiness. Ordering by LipE alone puts the old peptides back on top: the nonapeptide NH2-Lys-Thr-Glu-Glu-Ile-Ser-Glu-Val-Asn scores LipE 13.56 at an IC50 of 30 nM, because its cLogP of −6 inflates the difference, while its LE is 0.09. Charged, very polar molecules will always look good on LipE. The two metrics constrain different failure modes — too big, too greasy — and neither is a ranking of quality on its own.

The clinical candidates (Figure 2)

Only two BACE1 clinical candidates are recorded in BindingDB under their generic names: verubecestat (LE 0.42, LipE 7.26) and elenbecestat (LE 0.34, LipE 4.31). Both sit well inside the efficient region of the 2015–2019 panel in Figure 1. They are drawn in Figure 2 next to the patent record holder; all three carry a cyclic amidine that binds the catalytic aspartates and the same fluoroaryl amide arm. Both candidates failed in phase 3 — verubecestat for lack of efficacy, elenbecestat after an unfavourable risk-benefit assessment. Efficiency metrics say nothing about whether the target is the right one.

Most other candidates (lanabecestat, atabecestat, umibecestat) have no entry under those names at all: BindingDB records compounds under the name used in the source paper or patent, which is usually a code or a ChEMBL id.

How this was answered

  1. search_targets found human BACE1 (target 5093, 10,964 compounds).
  2. find_ligands_for_target with max_value_nm: 100 and order_by: "ligand_efficiency" ranked the potent inhibitors by efficiency, first without and then with properties: {min_heavy_atoms: 20}; order_by: "lipe" gave the lipophilic-efficiency ranking. Both metrics are computed from the compound_property table, which holds the RDKit heavy-atom count and cLogP of each compound's parent structure.
  3. A run_sql query took each compound's best exact value and the year it first appeared, then reported the median heavy-atom count, potency and LE per period, with the share above LE 0.3.
  4. The same per-compound values were plotted with matplotlib and added with add_figure; draw_structure_grid drew the three structures.
