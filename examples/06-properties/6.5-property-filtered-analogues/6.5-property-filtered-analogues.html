👤 User

I want analogues of ibrutinib that are potent on BTK but smaller and free of problem groups. Search by similarity with property filters and show me what survives.

🧠 Assistant

Filtering works, but on this target the obvious filter set throws away the mechanism. Here is what each step removes.

Search Similar compounds with a BTK value ≤ 10 nM
similarity_search, Tanimoto ≥ 0.5 282
+ max_mw: 450, exclude_alerts: ["pains"] 65
+ exclude_alerts: ["pains", "brenk"] 5

The first filter is cheap, the second is not. Capping molecular weight at 450 and dropping PAINS matches cuts 282 analogues to 65 — almost all of that from the weight cap, since only 89 of the 6,314 potent BTK compounds match any PAINS filter at all. Adding the Brenk filters cuts 65 to 5, and what it removes is the entire covalent series: 2,324 of those 6,314 compounds carry Michael_acceptor_1, and ibrutinib is one of them. Its acrylamide is exactly what the filter is designed to catch, and exactly how the drug works — it forms a covalent bond to Cys481 in the BTK active site. The NIH filters flag the same group as acrylate.

So for a covalent target, exclude PAINS but not Brenk, or you filter out the chemistry you came for. The reverse is also true: if you want reversible analogues, the Brenk filter is the fastest way to find them, which is what the five survivors are.

The five that survive (Figure 1)

All five keep ibrutinib's 4-amino-pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine hinge binder and its phenoxyphenyl arm, and replace the acrylamide-bearing piperidine with something inert — a plain cyclopentyl, a cyclohexanol, or a propanamide that cannot accept a Michael addition.

They are all 2–8 nM, about 30–100-fold weaker than ibrutinib's best reported 0.08 nM — but that comparison is unfair in a specific way. A covalent inhibitor's IC50 depends on how long the enzyme and compound are pre-incubated, because the covalent bond keeps forming; a reversible compound's does not. Comparing a reversible IC50 with a covalent IC50 measures the assay protocol as much as the compounds.

Caveats on the filters themselves

  • The weight cap is on the RDKit weight of the parent (salts and solvents removed), from compound_property, which is why it can differ from compound.mol_weight, BindingDB's weight for the whole record.
  • exclude_alerts drops a compound if it matches any alert in the named catalog. The catalogs are unequal in temperament: PAINS has 480 patterns aimed at assay interference, Brenk has 105 aimed at reactive or unstable groups in a lead, and the NIH set overlaps both. Brenk fires on roughly 47% of BTK's potent compounds and PAINS on 1.4%, so "exclude alerts" means something very different depending on which you pass.
  • Similarity is Tanimoto on Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 2048 bits) of the largest fragment, and 0.5 is a loose threshold: the five survivors run from 0.54 to 0.68.

How this was answered

  1. search_compounds found ibrutinib (BDBM50357312, 454 measurements) and search_targets found human BTK (target 1846, 13,926 compounds; the C481S resistance mutant is a separate target).
  2. A run_sql query read ibrutinib's own row in compound_property: MW 440.51, cLogP 4.22, QED 0.47, Brenk Michael_acceptor_1, NIH acrylate and halo_acrylate.
  3. similarity_search with threshold: 0.5, target_id: 1846 and max_value_nm: 10, run three times — unfiltered, then with properties: {max_mw: 450, exclude_alerts: ["pains"]}, then with brenk added. The property filter is applied before the result limit, so the counts are the real counts, not a filtered page.
  4. A run_sql query counted how many of BTK's potent compounds carry each alert class.
  5. draw_structure_grid drew ibrutinib with three of the survivors, aligned on the shared hinge binder.
